# 1. Editorial Purpose

This source map provides a controlled foundation for developing the contemplative content of **Path of Seeing**.

It is designed to help Qunara:

* ground reflections in identifiable Buddhist teachings
* distinguish early canonical material from later teacher interpretation
* avoid fabricated or misattributed quotations
* preserve important differences between traditions
* create original contemplative language without presenting editorial writing as scripture
* maintain a clear record of copyright and quotation risk

This document does **not** authorize the automatic generation of reflection questions from source text.

Every future question should pass through a separate editorial review process.

---

# 2. Source Classification

## A. Canonical Teaching

Teachings contained in recognized Buddhist textual collections.

For this map, the primary canonical collection is the early Buddhist discourses preserved through SuttaCentral.

Canonical status does not mean that every available English translation is free of copyright restrictions. The ancient source text and the modern translation must be treated separately.

---

## B. Teacher’s Direct Teaching

Teachings given directly by an identified Buddhist teacher, including:

* His Holiness the 14th Dalai Lama
* Thích Nhất Hạnh
* recognized Plum Village Dharma teachers where clearly attributed

These teachings may represent a specific tradition, interpretation, vocabulary, or pedagogical approach.

They must not automatically be presented as the universal Buddhist position.

---

## C. Modern Institutional Commentary

Explanations, articles, podcasts, summaries, interviews, or practice instructions published by an official Buddhist institution.

These may faithfully communicate a tradition while not being direct canonical statements or direct words of a named teacher.

---

## D. Qunara Editorial Interpretation

New language developed by Qunara after studying a source.

This layer may include:

* contemplative framing
* visual metaphors
* reflection directions
* thematic connections
* simplified language for general audiences
* relationships between multiple teachings

Qunara editorial interpretation must never be presented as:

* a quotation from the Buddha
* a quotation from the Dalai Lama
* a quotation from Thích Nhất Hạnh
* a canonical Buddhist formula
* the definitive interpretation of Buddhism

# 3. Quotation and Copyright Policy

## SuttaCentral

SuttaCentral states that its original material is dedicated to the public domain through **CC0 1.0**, while individual editions and translations should still be checked for their displayed license and attribution details. Bhikkhu Sujato’s editions commonly include an explicit copyright waiver.

**Editorial rule:**

* Prefer paraphrase even when quotation is legally permitted.
* Confirm the license shown on the exact translation page.
* Record the translator.
* Never combine lines from separate translations and present them as one quotation.
* Do not silently modernize a direct quotation.

---

## Office of His Holiness the Dalai Lama

The official website displays an all-content copyright notice belonging to the Office of His Holiness the Dalai Lama.

**Editorial rule:**

* Treat substantial quotation as restricted unless written permission or a specific reuse policy is obtained.
* Use brief quotations only when necessary, accurately attributed, and legally reviewed.
* Prefer original Qunara paraphrases linked to the source.
* Do not place the Dalai Lama’s name beneath original Qunara wording in a way that implies quotation.

---

## Plum Village

Plum Village articles, talks, transcripts, poetry, recordings, chants, and translations should be presumed copyrighted unless a page explicitly provides a reuse license.

**Editorial rule:**

* Prefer careful paraphrase.
* Treat poems, chants, guided practices, translations, books, talk transcripts, and distinctive teaching formulations as particularly sensitive.
* Do not reproduce Plum Village gāthās, poems, breathing verses, or extended passages without permission.
* Link to the official source whenever a teaching materially informs Qunara content.

## SC-001

**Teacher or tradition:** Early Buddhism; Pāli Nikāyas
**Official source title:** *Dhammacakkappavattana Sutta — Rolling Forth the Wheel of Dhamma*
**Canonical reference:** SN 56.11
**Source type:** Canonical teaching
**Key themes:** Suffering, dissatisfaction, attachment, cessation, ethical and contemplative practice

**Careful summary:**
The discourse presents suffering as something to be understood rather than merely feared or eliminated. It identifies craving as a central condition in the arising of suffering and describes cessation and a path of practice. “Suffering” here should not be reduced to ordinary sadness. It includes the instability and unsatisfying nature of experiences that cannot be secured permanently.

**Possible reflection directions:**

* noticing what feels difficult without immediately resisting it
* observing the difference between pain and the struggle surrounding pain
* recognizing wanting, rejecting, and holding
* noticing whether satisfaction depends on unstable conditions
* exploring what changes when grasping relaxes

**Direct quotation:** Permitted only according to the license of the selected translation
**Copyright caution:** Verify translator-specific license; do not treat a modern translation as copyright-free merely because the underlying discourse is ancient
**URL:** https://suttacentral.net/sn56.11/en/sujato
**Review status:** Approved as foundational canonical source

## SC-002

**Teacher or tradition:** Early Buddhism; Pāli Nikāyas
**Official source title:** *Anattalakkhaṇa Sutta — The Characteristic of Not-Self*
**Canonical reference:** SN 22.59
**Source type:** Canonical teaching
**Key themes:** Non-self, identity, impermanence, attachment, suffering

**Careful summary:**
The discourse examines body, feeling, perception, volitional activity, and consciousness. It asks whether these changing and afflictive processes can reasonably be regarded as fully controllable, permanent, or as an enduring self. The teaching should not be simplified into “you do not exist.” Its method challenges identification and ownership in relation to conditioned experience.

**Possible reflection directions:**

* noticing what is being called “me” in the present moment
* observing whether a feeling obeys the command to remain
* distinguishing experience from ownership of experience
* noticing the difference between identity and changing processes
* exploring what becomes less rigid when identification softens

**Direct quotation:** Translation-license dependent
**Copyright caution:** Avoid isolated phrases that falsely imply nihilism or denial of personhood
**URL:** https://suttacentral.net/sn22.59/en/sujato
**Review status:** Approved with doctrinal-sensitivity review required

## SC-003

**Teacher or tradition:** Early Buddhism; Pāli Nikāyas
**Official source title:** *Paṭiccasamuppāda Sutta — Dependent Origination*
**Canonical reference:** SN 12.1
**Source type:** Canonical teaching
**Key themes:** Dependent origination, conditions, suffering, attachment, change

**Careful summary:**
The discourse presents suffering as arising through conditions rather than from a single isolated cause or a fixed essence. The traditional sequence links ignorance, activity, consciousness, sense experience, feeling, craving, grasping, becoming, birth, and suffering. It also presents cessation through the ending of supporting conditions.

**Possible reflection directions:**

* observing what conditions are supporting a present reaction
* noticing the transition from sensation to wanting
* seeing that an emotion is conditioned rather than self-created in isolation
* tracing how one response gives rise to another
* recognizing that changing conditions may change an experience

**Direct quotation:** Translation-license dependent
**Copyright caution:** Do not reduce dependent origination to the vague claim that “everything is connected”
**URL:** https://suttacentral.net/sn12.1/en/sujato
**Review status:** Approved as foundational; specialist review required for detailed sequence

## SC-004

**Canonical reference:** SN 12.2

## SC-006

**Teacher or tradition:** Early Buddhism; Pāli Nikāyas
**Official source title:** *Satipaṭṭhāna Sutta — Mindfulness Meditation*
**Canonical reference:** MN 10
**Source type:** Canonical teaching and practice framework
**Key themes:** Mindfulness, body, feeling, mind, impermanence, suffering

**Careful summary:**
The discourse organizes mindfulness around observation of the body, feelings, mind, and experiential patterns. Observation includes arising and passing, internal and external experience, and the presence or absence of particular mental qualities. Mindfulness is not presented as passive relaxation alone; it functions within a broader path of ethical and liberating practice.

**Possible reflection directions:**

* recognizing what is present without immediately adding a story
* noticing whether a feeling is pleasant, painful, or neutral
* observing the arising and passing of a mental state
* distinguishing direct experience from interpretation
* returning attention to the body as an anchor

**Direct quotation:** Translation-license dependent
**Copyright caution:** Do not market mindfulness as a neutral productivity technique detached from ethical context
**URL:** https://suttacentral.net/mn10/en/sujato
**Review status:** Approved as foundational practice source

## SC-007

**Teacher or tradition:** Early Buddhism; Pāli Nikāyas
**Official source title:** *Ānāpānassati Sutta — Mindfulness of Breathing*
**Canonical reference:** MN 118
**Source type:** Canonical meditation teaching
**Key themes:** Breathing, mindfulness, body, feeling, mind, impermanence, letting go

**Careful summary:**
The discourse describes a progressive practice of knowing the breath, experiencing the body, calming bodily activity, attending to feeling and mind, and contemplating impermanence, fading, cessation, and relinquishment. Breathing is part of a complete contemplative development, not merely a calming exercise.

**Possible reflection directions:**

* simply knowing an in-breath as an in-breath
* noticing the beginning, continuation, and ending of a breath
* observing how the body changes during breathing
* recognizing that no breath can be retained
* sensing release without forcing a result

**Direct quotation:** Translation-license dependent
**Copyright caution:** Do not convert the sequence into medical breathing advice or guaranteed physiological claims
**URL:** https://suttacentral.net/mn118/en/sujato
**Review status:** Approved as primary breathing source

## SC-008

**Teacher or tradition:** Early Buddhism; Pāli Nikāyas
**Official source title:** *Mahāhatthipadopama Sutta — The Greater Discourse on the Simile of the Elephant’s Footprint*
**Canonical reference:** MN 28
**Source type:** Canonical teaching
**Key themes:** Conditions, non-self, body, feeling, interdependence

**Careful summary:**
The discourse analyzes embodied experience through elements, contact, and conditioned mental processes. It repeatedly undermines the idea that physical or mental events arise independently. It also uses the standard contemplative formula that phenomena are not appropriately possessed as “mine,” identified as “I,” or taken as a permanent self.

**Possible reflection directions:**

* observing how an experience depends on body, contact, memory, and attention
* noticing what is dependent rather than independent
* exploring the limits of ownership over bodily experience
* recognizing multiple conditions within a single moment

**Direct quotation:** Translation-license dependent
**Copyright caution:** Do not equate this discourse directly with Plum Village “interbeing” without marking the latter as a modern teaching formulation
**URL:** https://suttacentral.net/mn28/en/sujato
**Review status:** Approved

# 7. Sources Requiring Extra Caution

## Anger

Questions must not:

* shame the user for feeling anger
* imply that anger is always irrational
* encourage suppression
* suggest remaining in danger
* require compassion toward an aggressor during an unsafe situation

---

## Forgiveness

Questions must not:

* command forgiveness
* imply moral failure if forgiveness is not possible
* equate forgiveness with reconciliation
* encourage renewed contact
* minimize abuse or injustice

---

## Non-Self

Questions must not:

* say the user is unreal
* encourage dissociation
* deny ordinary personal identity
* suggest that responsibility disappears
* present non-self as nihilism

---

## Suffering

Questions must not:

* intensify distress
* ask users to revisit traumatic memories
* imply that all suffering is caused by personal attachment
* blame users for illness, grief, oppression, or harm

---

## Death

Questions must remain gentle, optional, and non-graphic.

They must not:

* romanticize death
* speculate about a user’s death
* imply that fear of death is spiritual failure
* assert metaphysical claims as facts
* appear unexpectedly in early session stages

---

## Breathing

Breathing guidance must not:

* require breath retention
* prescribe a medical breathing rate
* promise anxiety reduction or healing
* imply failure when synchronization is difficult
* prevent users from breathing naturally

# 9. Review Status Definitions

**Approved as foundational**
Suitable for direct use in the editorial research process.

**Approved**
Authoritative and relevant, but must still follow copyright and contextual review.

**Approved with doctrinal review**
Requires review by a qualified Buddhist studies or tradition-informed editor before publication.

**Approved with safety restrictions**
May inform content only when the specified emotional and ethical safeguards are applied.

**Thematic source only**
May guide themes but should not be closely paraphrased or structurally copied.

**Pending copyright review**
No direct quotation may be published until usage rights are resolved.

**Rejected**
Must not be used in the Path of Seeing content system.

# 10. Final Editorial Rule

Path of Seeing should not speak with borrowed authority.

Its questions should be original, quiet, and transparent in origin.

The proper development sequence is:

1. study the authoritative source
2. identify the teaching accurately
3. record its tradition and context
4. determine the safe contemplative direction
5. write an original Qunara reflection
6. compare the reflection against the source
7. check for doctrinal distortion
8. check for emotional and clinical risk
9. check for accidental quotation
10. approve, revise, or reject

The final experience should feel simple.

The research behind it should remain rigorous.
